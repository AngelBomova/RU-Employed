# Interview assets

`rachel.glb` and `male.glb` derive from **Female_Adult_01** and **Male_Adult_01** in the [Microsoft Rocketbox Avatar Library](https://github.com/microsoft/Microsoft-Rocketbox), copyright (c) 2020 Microsoft, distributed under the **MIT license**. The complete license is retained in [Microsoft-Rocketbox-LICENSE.md](./Microsoft-Rocketbox-LICENSE.md). Source revision: `0943055db6ec570bcef9f2c8b41c9e5467c808f9`.

The source files are `Assets/Avatars/Adults/Female_Adult_01/Export/Female_Adult_01_facial.fbx` and `Assets/Avatars/Adults/Male_Adult_01/Export/Male_Adult_01_facial.fbx`, with the matching body, head and opacity textures from each character's `Textures` directory. RU Employed converts them to self-contained glTF binaries, normalizes the scale, lowers the arms into a relaxed pose, uses PBR materials, resizes embedded textures to 1024 pixels, and retains ten facial blend shapes for blinking, a gentle smile and speech. The face, hair, neck, clothing and eyes retain the original connected geometry and skin rig. Microsoft does not endorse RU Employed.

`face_landmarker.task` comes from Google's [official MediaPipe model distribution](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-models/face_landmarker/face_landmarker/float16/1/face_landmarker.task), as documented in the [Face Landmarker guide](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/face_landmarker). Frames are processed locally and discarded. The MediaPipe runtime is distributed under Apache-2.0; three.js uses MIT. Dependency licenses are included in their installed packages.
